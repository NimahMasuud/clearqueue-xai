# ClearQueue: explainable complaint triage for public services

**Challenge:** 04, Explain Which Public Complaint Needs Priority (Track 3: Explainable AI)

**Participant:** Nimatallahi Masuud Kehinde (individual) | GitHub: github.com/NimahMasuud | LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/nimah-masuud-kehinde

**Event:** AI Innovation Hackathon 2026, Women in AI Research, Innovation & Entrepreneurship Club

## Problem
Public offices receive more complaints than staff can read closely. A model can sort them, but if nobody can see why a
complaint was marked low priority, staff cannot check the call and citizens have no reason to trust it.

## What it does
You paste a complaint. ClearQueue returns a priority (High, Medium, Low), the department or queue that should handle it,
a one-sentence reason, the words that drove the result, and the smallest wording change that would flip the priority.
Cases where the model is unsure are flagged for a person.

## Key features
- Priority and department prediction from complaint text
- Contrastive reason: why High rather than Medium. The sentence is built from the model's own top features
- Local explanation with LIME, and a second view from the model's coefficients
- Global view of the strongest terms per priority level and department
- Counterfactual: minimal word removal that changes the priority
- Human-review flag on low-confidence cases
- Evaluation focused on missed High-priority cases and false-priority errors

## Stack
Python, pandas, scikit-learn (TF-IDF, logistic regression, linear SVM, random forest), LIME, Streamlit.

## Workflow
complaint text -> TF-IDF (words and word pairs) -> two logistic regression models (priority, department)
-> explanation layer (coefficients, LIME, contrast sentence, counterfactual) -> dashboard.

## Dataset
Synthetic complaints in a 311 style for Nigerian public services, generated by `src/make_data.py` (3,000 rows, fixed seed).
Seven queues: Water & Sanitation, Power Distribution, Roads & Transport, Waste Management, Emergency & Public Safety,
Health Services, and an Electoral Services Desk for routine election-period service requests (voter card, voter list,
registration centre).
The Stage 1 proposal mentioned open 311-style data. For the build I used a documented synthetic set instead, which the challenge allows.

**Severity rubric.** High: immediate risk to life, health or safety, or a threat to people. Medium: an essential service is
disrupted or a problem is growing, with no immediate danger. Low: inconvenience, billing or information. A complaint
that involves a vulnerable setting (school, health centre, market, children, elderly people) moves up one level.
About 6% of labels are shifted by one level on purpose to mimic reviewer disagreement. Place names, openers and
filler sentences ("This is urgent.") are random and do not depend on the label.

## Models
Priority: logistic regression (served and explained), linear SVM and random forest (comparison only).
Department: logistic regression. Split: 80/20, stratified, seed 42.

## Results
Run `python src/train.py` and see `documentation/results.md` and `models/metrics.json`. Numbers from my last run:
priority macro F1 about 0.94 (logistic regression), about 3.5% of High-priority complaints missed, about 3.5% of High
predictions false, 99.7% of predictions unchanged when filler wording changes, and 66% word overlap between the
coefficient view and LIME on 40 test complaints. Department accuracy is 100% because the synthetic phrases are
clean; I do not expect that on real data.

## Limitations
- The data is synthetic, so scores are higher than real-world scores would be. Labels come from a rubric I wrote.
- Wording far from the training phrases lowers accuracy; vague complaints are flagged for review.
- Explanations show how the model behaves, not what causes a problem.
- Subgroup checks cover complaint length and per-department recall only. No real demographic data was used.
- About a fifth of test complaints are flagged for human review at the current thresholds; they can be tuned in `src/explain.py`.
- The model learns the rubric I wrote, not real-world ground truth. Real deployment would need labels from trained complaint officers.
- High-priority recall is lower for Waste Management (0.80) and the Electoral Services Desk (0.88), where High examples are rare.
- The model treats the word "wrong" as a Low-priority signal because it appears mostly in billing complaints in the synthetic data. A dangerous complaint using that word could be underrated.
- Human review is flagged when priority confidence is below 75% or department confidence is below 60%.
  
## Ethics
No personal data. Staff stay accountable: the tool supports triage and never decides alone. The electoral category covers
routine service requests only, with no political content.

## Future scope
Real complaint data from an open portal (for example NYC 311) for comparison, SHAP as a third explanation method,
Pidgin and Yoruba text, a FastAPI endpoint, and a user study on whether staff find the reasons useful.
